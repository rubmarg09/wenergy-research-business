# Wenergy — Go Green Challenge 2026

## Application Q01 — Problem

### What problem are you trying to solve?

Local territories already possess significant renewable and recoverable-energy potential, public datasets, climate plans, infrastructure knowledge and professional expertise. Yet these elements remain fragmented across tools, organisations, studies and datasets.

This creates a **Territorial Energy Execution-Intelligence Gap**.

A municipality or intermunicipal authority may know that solar, biomass, geothermal, waste heat, hydro, biogas or other resources exist locally, but still struggle to answer:

> **Which concrete project relationships deserve professional investigation first, and why?**

The problem is not simply a lack of data.

It is the difficulty of connecting:

- local energy resources;
- actual or estimated energy demand;
- buildings and infrastructure;
- public and private actors;
- planning and environmental constraints;
- technical evidence;
- project maturity;
- professional next steps.

The result is a fragmented decision process in which local authorities can spend significant time searching through different tools, commissioning studies, coordinating actors and rebuilding information that may already exist elsewhere.

This problem is particularly important for smaller and rural territories with limited internal engineering, GIS and energy-planning capacity.

The Pays Foix-Varilhes territory illustrates this challenge. It includes 42 communes and approximately 33,679 inhabitants across around 444 km². Foix itself has a poverty rate of approximately 24.0%, while the wider intermunicipal territory has a poverty rate of approximately 16.1%. This means that energy transition decisions must not only be technically sound, but also understandable, accessible and relevant to communities with unequal resources.

Wenergy therefore focuses on a simple but operational question:

> **How can a territory move from fragmented local energy information to a small, explainable and evidence-backed shortlist of projects worth deeper professional investigation?**

The current Wenergy POC has already demonstrated the scale of the challenge by structuring:

- 32,628 screening opportunities;
- 38,069 actors;
- 32,628 Project Lenses;
- 8 energy pathways;
- 85,734 building polygons.

However:

> **SCREENING ≠ FEASIBILITY**

The objective is not to maximise the number of opportunities.

It is to reduce a very large territorial screening universe into a smaller, transparent and actionable project pipeline.

### Core problem statement

> **Territories do not necessarily lack energy potential or public data. They lack a shared intelligence and orchestration layer capable of connecting fragmented evidence, actors, local needs, energy resources and constraints into an actionable project-development pipeline.**

## Key Evidence

- 1,252 EPCIs with own taxation existed in France at 1 January 2026.
- More than 80% of the French population lived in an EPCI with a PCAET by February 2026.
- French local authorities face substantial climate-investment needs and limited implementation capacity.
- Wenergy's current Foix POC contains 32,628 screening opportunities across 8 pathways.

---

## Claims Boundary

Wenergy does **not** claim that:

- 32,628 opportunities are feasible projects;
- all mapped actors wish to participate;
- all mapped relationships reflect ownership;
- screening replaces engineering;
- the current POC proves commercial adoption.

The POC demonstrates a real data and product architecture.

The next step is real-world validation.
