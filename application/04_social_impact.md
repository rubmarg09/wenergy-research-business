# Wenergy — Go Green Challenge 2026

## Application Q04 — Social Impact

### Primary Social Impact

> **Inclusive territorial energy access, community empowerment and local energy decision capacity.**

Wenergy is built around the idea that energy intelligence should not remain accessible only to large cities, specialist consultants, energy developers or highly technical users.

Smaller territories and local communities should also be able to understand:

- what energy resources exist;
- what opportunities may be realistic;
- what evidence supports them;
- what remains uncertain;
- who is involved;
- what happens next.

---

## The Social Problem

Energy-transition information is fragmented across:

- GIS systems;
- specialist studies;
- regulatory tools;
- public datasets;
- consultant reports;
- institutional knowledge.

A resident, farmer, small commune or local business may have little ability to interpret this information.

Therefore:

> **MORE ENERGY DATA DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY CREATE MORE ENERGY ACCESS.**

---

## Initial Population Reach

The Pays Foix-Varilhes pilot territory contains approximately:

> **33,679 inhabitants**

across:

> **42 communes**

This places the initial population-served category above 10,000 people.

---

## B2G2C Access Model

Wenergy's proposed B2G2C model allows:

> **THE TERRITORY TO FUND CORE ACCESS**

rather than requiring every resident to purchase an individual subscription.

Potential community-facing functions include:

- simplified Project Lens views;
- evidence explanations;
- project maturity;
- updates;
- participation opportunities.

---

## Simple Mode and Expert Mode

### Simple Mode

Designed for accessibility.

It explains:

- what the opportunity is;
- why it matters;
- what is known;
- what is uncertain;
- what happens next.

### Expert Mode

Provides:

- methodology;
- evidence provenance;
- assumptions;
- constraints;
- technical detail.

Both should rely on the same underlying evidence.

---

## Small-Commune Inclusion

Small communes may lack:

- dedicated energy teams;
- GIS specialists;
- procurement expertise;
- engineering staff.

A shared territorial licence can allow smaller communes to benefit through a larger intermunicipal buyer.

---

## Local Actor Participation

Potential contributors include:

- residents;
- farmers;
- businesses;
- facility operators;
- local authorities;
- professionals.

Local actors can help close evidence gaps.

Example:

> Wenergy identifies missing biomass availability data.

↓

> Farmer confirms availability.

↓

> Evidence status changes.

↓

> Project Lens confidence improves.

This turns participation into operational evidence rather than symbolic consultation.

---

## Equity and Distribution

Future project assessment should ask:

- Who pays?
- Who benefits?
- Who owns?
- Who bears risk?
- Are benefits geographically distributed?
- Are vulnerable groups reached?

Wenergy does not assume that a technically viable local energy project automatically produces equitable benefits.

---

## Social Impact Boundary

Wenergy does not currently claim verified:

- household energy savings;
- energy-poverty reduction;
- local employment creation;
- affordability improvement.

These are possible downstream outcomes that require implementation and measurement.

---

## Social Impact Thesis

Wenergy's social value is not simply that residents can view a map.

It is that more people and smaller territories can participate in local energy development with:

- better information;
- clearer explanations;
- visible uncertainty;
- appropriate participation;
- stronger connection to real professional action.
