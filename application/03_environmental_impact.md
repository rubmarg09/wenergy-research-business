# Wenergy — Go Green Challenge 2026

## Application Q03 — Environmental Impact

### Environmental impact approach

Wenergy does not directly install renewable-energy infrastructure.

Its environmental impact is therefore primarily **enabling**.

The intended causal chain is:

> **BETTER TERRITORIAL EVIDENCE**

↓

> **BETTER RESOURCE / NEED MATCHING**

↓

> **BETTER PROJECT PRIORITISATION**

↓

> **BETTER PROFESSIONAL INVESTIGATION**

↓

> **MORE VIABLE PROJECTS ADVANCE**

↓

> **REAL RENEWABLE / RECOVERED ENERGY INFRASTRUCTURE**

↓

> **LOWER FOSSIL ENERGY USE AND EMISSIONS**

The current POC can measure upstream enabling indicators such as:

- screening opportunities;
- evidence coverage;
- pathway distribution;
- project maturity;
- actor context;
- constraint context;
- next-action completeness.

It cannot yet legitimately claim verified downstream outcomes such as:

- MWh generated;
- MWh of waste heat recovered;
- fossil energy displaced;
- verified CO2 avoided.

Those outcomes require professional validation and implemented projects.

## Multi-Energy Environmental Value

Wenergy currently supports eight pathways:

- solar;
- waste heat;
- biomass;
- hydro;
- geothermal;
- wind;
- agricultural biogas;
- municipal waste / recoverable energy.

The core principle is:

> **THE TERRITORY SHOULD HELP DETERMINE THE TECHNOLOGY — NOT THE TECHNOLOGY DETERMINE THE TERRITORY.**

This allows territories to compare energy options instead of starting from one predetermined technology.

---

## Recoverable Energy and Circularity

Wenergy can help identify relationships where local energy flows are currently wasted.

Examples include:

- industrial or commercial waste heat;
- biomass residues;
- agricultural residues;
- municipal recoverable-energy streams.

However, Wenergy does not assume energy recovery is always the best circular option.

Avoidance, reuse and material recovery must remain part of the wider hierarchy.

---

## Digital Environmental Footprint

Wenergy also recognises that digital systems have a footprint.

The architecture therefore follows a:

> **DETERMINISTIC-FIRST**

approach wherever practical.

High-volume screening should rely on:

- deterministic algorithms;
- geospatial computation;
- published factors;
- statistical models;
- cached outputs.

Generative AI is intended mainly for:

- explanation;
- extraction;
- translation;
- summarisation;
- structuring.

Core rule:

> **LLM OUTPUT ≠ OBSERVED FACT**

The product aims to maximise:

> **DECISION VALUE PER UNIT OF COMPUTE**

rather than maximise AI usage.

## Environmental Impact Maturity

Wenergy separates:

1. screening potential;
2. evidence-backed candidate;
3. professional feasibility;
4. funded/permitted project;
5. construction;
6. operating asset;
7. measured energy;
8. verified emissions impact.

Current POC maturity is concentrated at the early stages.

---

## Anti-Greenwashing Rule

> **POTENTIAL ≠ ACHIEVED IMPACT**

Every future impact value should show whether it is:

- potential;
- estimated;
- expert-validated;
- measured.

This prevents modelled environmental potential from being presented as realised impact.
