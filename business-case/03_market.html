A structured institutional market with a real execution problem.

Wenergy's initial market is not "everyone who uses energy". It starts with territorial public organisations that already carry climate, energy and infrastructure responsibilities — but often lack a shared layer for comparing opportunities and organising action.

Why Now

The market is being pushed from planning toward implementation.

01

Climate plans already exist

Territorial climate strategy is increasingly institutionalised through structures such as PCAET.

02

Public buildings create real demand

Energy renovation, tertiary obligations and heating decarbonisation create practical investment questions.

03

Renewable / recoverable heat is funded

Public programmes such as Fonds Chaleur create implementation pathways when viable projects are identified.

04

Local capacity remains constrained

Small territories cannot continuously perform deep multi-pathway analysis across every local asset and actor.

THE MARKET DRIVER IS NOT "MORE DATA". IT IS THE NEED TO TURN EXISTING CLIMATE RESPONSIBILITIES INTO IMPLEMENTABLE PROJECTS.
Ideal Customer Profile

Start where the execution gap is strongest.

The strongest first customer is not necessarily the largest public authority. It is the organisation where climate responsibility, project complexity and limited analytical capacity overlap.

Best Initial Fit

Small / Medium EPCI

Territorial buyer responsible for multiple communes and climate/energy coordination, but without a large permanent project-intelligence team.

  • Active PCAET / energy agenda
  • Multiple communes
  • Mixed local resources
  • Limited internal engineering
  • Need to prioritise studies
  • Identifiable internal champion
High-Leverage

Energy Syndicate

Potential buyer and distribution layer able to coordinate energy intelligence across multiple member territories.

  • Energy expertise
  • Multi-territory reach
  • Existing institutional trust
  • Potential channel leverage
  • Professional handoff capacity
Expansion

Agglomeration / Larger Territory

More sophisticated buyer where value may come from cross-department orchestration, portfolio memory and complex project relationships.

  • More existing tools
  • Higher coordination complexity
  • Greater integration requirements
  • Potential higher ACV
  • Higher competitive threshold

Buying Triggers

Wenergy becomes relevant when a territory has to decide what deserves attention.

New PCAET / climate-programme cycle Need to move from strategic goals into project portfolios.
New funding opportunity Need to rapidly identify credible candidate projects.
Public-building energy challenge Need to compare local decarbonisation pathways.
Waste-heat / local resource discovery Need to find suitable nearby demand and actors.
Study budget constraint Need to decide which projects justify professional feasibility.
Institutional transition Need to preserve project intelligence when teams or leadership change.

Poor-Fit Customer

Who should Wenergy avoid initially?

Early GTM should avoid accounts where:

  • no climate or energy project agenda exists;
  • no internal champion can own the workflow;
  • existing internal tools already solve the exact problem well;
  • procurement is impossible within the startup runway;
  • the buyer expects Wenergy to replace engineering feasibility;
  • the territory only wants a one-off static map with no recurring use.
A LARGE ADDRESSABLE MARKET DOES NOT MEAN EVERY TERRITORY IS A GOOD CUSTOMER.
THE INITIAL COMMERCIAL QUESTION IS NOT "HOW MANY TERRITORIES EXIST?" It is: which territories experience enough project-prioritisation pain to pay for a shared intelligence layer — and keep using it after the first scan?
