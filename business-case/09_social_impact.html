Chapter 09 / 12

Energy intelligence should not be reserved for the territories with the largest teams and budgets.

Wenergy's social mission is to expand territorial energy access, community understanding and local decision capacity while preserving the professional and governance boundaries required for responsible project development.

Territorial Equity Community Access Rural Inclusion Public Value
Social Problem

Energy transition information is difficult to use.

Local energy intelligence can be spread across:

  • GIS systems;
  • consultant reports;
  • specialist datasets;
  • regulatory platforms;
  • technical studies;
  • institutional knowledge.
SOCIAL ACCESS GAP
Wenergy Response

Turn fragmented information into shared territorial intelligence.

Make project evidence easier to:

  • understand;
  • compare;
  • challenge;
  • contribute to;
  • connect to action.
MORE ENERGY DATA DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY CREATE MORE ENERGY ACCESS.
Social Impact Chain

Information becomes valuable when people can use it.

1 Accessible Information Evidence is presented in understandable form.

3 Participation Relevant actors can contribute evidence.
4 Connection Projects connect to actors and professionals.
5 Decision Capacity Territories make stronger choices.

Distributional Equity

A successful project is not automatically an equitable project.

Wenergy's future Community Benefit Lens should help territories ask how costs, ownership, risk and benefits are distributed.

Who Pays? Public authority, business, household or investor?
Who Saves? Which users experience cost reductions?
Who Owns? Who captures long-term asset value?
Who Bears Risk? Financial, planning, operational or social risk?
Who Participates? Are relevant groups able to understand and engage?
Community Benefit Lens

Social value can become part of project comparison.

Beneficiaries Which groups receive value?
Affordability Does the project improve access or cost?
Local Ownership Is local ownership relevant or possible?
Geographic Equity Which communes receive investment?
Engagement Need What level of participation is appropriate?
Digital Inclusion

Digital access should not become a new barrier.

Mobile Usability Community-facing access should work on ordinary devices.
Clear Language Avoid unnecessary technical jargon in Simple Mode.
Low-Bandwidth Design Lightweight pages and cached content support weaker connectivity.
Multilingual Potential Translation can support deployment and inclusion across territories.
Institutional Empowerment

Social impact also means strengthening the public organisations themselves.

A territory that can preserve evidence, compare projects, explain priorities and retain institutional project memory is better equipped to manage the energy transition over time.

Social Guardrails

Participation and access must not be overstated.

ACCESS ≠ EMPOWERMENT
PARTICIPATION ≠ CONTROL
INTEREST ≠ TECHNICAL PRIORITY
LOCAL ENERGY ≠ AUTOMATIC AFFORDABILITY
IMPLEMENTATION ≠ EQUITABLE BENEFIT
COMMUNITY POPULARITY ≠ TECHNICAL VALIDITY
WENERGY'S SOCIAL VALUE IS NOT SIMPLY THAT RESIDENTS CAN VIEW A MAP. It is that more people and smaller territories can become part of the local energy-development process with better information, clearer explanations, visible uncertainty, appropriate participation and stronger connection to professional action.
